Autonomous Driving Platform: a self-driving vehicle prototype (capstone)

Team capstone project, January to August 2021

Summary

A self-driving vehicle prototype built as a team capstone for the Mechatronic Systems Engineering programme at Simon Fraser University. It uses computer vision and deep neural networks to steer and drive a vehicle, with a remote control that can override the AI at any time.

Overview

The project has four parts:

  1. Car hacking. The steering, throttle and brake potentiometers are replaced with MCP4725 digital-to-analogue converters driven by an Arduino Uno. A radio receiver passes the driver's transmitter signals to the Arduino, which maps them to voltages, so the car can be driven by hand or by the computer.
  2. Data collection. Over 70,000 driving frames were recorded by a laptop script that pairs camera images with steering and throttle values read over serial. About 1,100 images were hand-labelled in YOLO format for pedestrians, vehicles, stop signs and 30 km/h speed signs.
  3. AI training. A modified PilotNet (NVIDIA's end-to-end steering model) with 5 convolutional and 4 fully connected layers predicts steering and throttle from the camera image. Because the data was biased toward straight driving, excess near-zero-steering samples were removed and images were flipped to balance it. YOLOv4-Tiny detects the labelled objects.
  4. Integration and testing. The steering model and the object detector run together in a Python script, and the remote can take over at any time. Sidewalk tests covered vehicles, pedestrians, stop signs and speed signs.

Future work

  • Add at least three more cameras for 360-degree awareness.
  • Collect more training data.
  • Extend object detection to school zones, bicycles, one-way and merge signs.
  • Add an LSTM memory model alongside the CNN for time-dependent predictions.
